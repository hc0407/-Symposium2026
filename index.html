Symposium on Protein Language Models

Format Focused in-person symposium

Sequence, structure, text, and network evidence brought into a shared modeling space.

Theme

Protein language models for the next stage of protein science

Protein language models are changing how researchers represent protein sequences, structures, functions, and interactions. This symposium brings together researchers from China and Japan to discuss multimodal PLMs, robust evaluation frameworks, and applications in function annotation, protein interaction prediction, variant effect interpretation, clinical phenotype analysis, and drug discovery.

Model Methods

Advances in foundation model architectures, sequence-structure-text fusion, task adaptation, interpretability, and generative modeling.

Evaluation

Standardized, dynamically updated benchmarks that reduce information leakage and support fair comparison across models, tasks, and species.

Applications

Translational use cases in protein function annotation, PPI prediction, protein design, clinical phenotype prediction, and drug-target discovery.

Call for Abstracts

Share recent work, tools, datasets, and early-stage ideas

We invite abstract submissions from researchers and students working across bioinformatics, computational biology, biomedical AI, and related data-driven life science methods. Submissions may describe completed studies, ongoing projects, benchmark resources, software tools, datasets, application case studies, or methodological perspectives.

Topics of Interest

  • AI and machine learning methods for bioinformatics and computational biology
  • Protein, DNA, RNA, molecule, and multimodal foundation models
  • Sequence analysis, comparative genomics, and evolutionary bioinformatics
  • Structural bioinformatics, protein design, and molecular modeling
  • Functional annotation, interaction prediction, pathways, and biological networks
  • Single-cell, spatial, clinical, and multi-omics data analysis
  • Drug discovery, variant interpretation, phenotype prediction, and translational applications
  • Databases, benchmarks, reproducible workflows, visualization, and software tools

Abstract Format

  • Language: English
  • Length: up to 300 words
  • Include title, authors, affiliations, and corresponding email
  • Submit as PDF or Word document by email
  • Indicate preference for oral presentation or poster discussion
